# Heatwave Safety Guidelines

## What is a heatwave
A heatwave is a period of unusually high temperature. In South Asia, temperatures above 40°C, or 5°C above the normal for that time of year, are generally treated as heatwave conditions. High humidity makes the heat feel worse and more dangerous.

## Heat risk levels
- 32°C to 37°C: Caution. Fatigue is possible with prolonged exposure or activity.
- 38°C to 40°C: Extreme caution. Heat cramps and heat exhaustion are possible.
- 41°C to 45°C: Danger. Heat exhaustion is likely, and heatstroke is possible with prolonged exposure.
- Above 45°C: Extreme danger. Heatstroke is highly likely. Avoid going outside.

## Precautions during a heatwave
- Drink water regularly, even if you do not feel thirsty.
- Avoid outdoor activity between 11 AM and 4 PM, when the sun is strongest.
- Wear light-coloured, loose, cotton clothing and cover your head with a hat or cloth.
- Stay in shade or air-conditioned places whenever possible.
- Avoid heavy meals, tea, coffee, and sugary drinks, which can increase dehydration.
- Oral rehydration solution (ORS) or lemon water with a pinch of salt helps replace lost salts.

## Signs of heatstroke
Heatstroke is a medical emergency. Signs include body temperature above 40°C, hot dry or very sweaty skin, confusion, dizziness, rapid pulse, headache, nausea, and fainting. Move the person to a cool place, loosen their clothing, cool them with wet cloths or water, and call emergency services immediately.

## Vulnerable groups
Children, elderly people, pregnant women, outdoor workers, and people with chronic illness are at higher risk. Never leave children or pets inside a parked car.
